# Hygiene investigation

Investigate owner-selected scope or the dispatched work item. Read current
project/work-item state, dependencies, formal intent and Bridge attempt through
the CLI before relying on earlier observations. Use current help to locate the
relevant domain reader. Prior reports are investigation leads.

Identify the intended artifact/subsystem outcome and every live consumer:
source, schema, CLI behavior, tests, baseline instructions, templates and
derived surfaces. Follow behavior and terminology as well as exact filenames.
Compare observations with current formal intent and owner direction.
A clean search or small passing selection does not establish completeness.

A finding states its class (defect, debt, drift or decision-needed), title,
evidence, affected scope, impact and concrete remaining work. Distinguish
implemented behavior, correct refusal, unexecuted obligation and obsolete
assertion. Use current formal artifact-lifecycle sources, including
DCL-ARTIFACT-LIFECYCLE-TRIGGERS-001 where applicable.

Group interdependent corrections into a project outcome that completes and
commits together. Programs order projects; each work item has one project and
describes a concrete artifact change or action. Check existing work before
proposing intake. Use established intake for unmatched defects; a report neither
creates membership nor authorizes work.

Keep temporary evidence in the current context's scratch space. Report bounded
coverage and uncertainty; do not repeat a census indefinitely to claim that
nothing remains. Read-only investigation does not mutate, bulk-close, commit
or purge its subject. Apply an owner decision directly to current SoT/action
when directed. Interactive logs retain the conversation.

Inspect only this harness's operational configuration. Neutral source and its
projector are engineering inputs; peer directories and historical role/capability
registries are neither authority nor dependencies.
